Safe Summer Footwear: Sandals That Protect Numb Feet

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Diabetic sandals with cushioned footbeds, adjustable straps, and protective toe and heel designs help shield numb feet from cuts, blisters, and pressure points that often go unnoticed and can lead to slow-healing wounds. Choosing the right pair, breaking them in properly, and following daily foot care routines are key to preventing serious complications.

Because diabetic foot issues can escalate quickly—and symptoms like numbness, tingling, or unhealed sores may signal underlying nerve or circulation problems—it's smart to understand what's happening before it worsens. Explanation

Summer brings warmth and sunshine—but for people with diabetes and diabetic neuropathy (numb feet), it also brings extra risk. Reduced sensation in the feet means you might not notice cuts, blisters or pressure points before they become serious. Choosing the right sandals can help you stay comfortable, supported and safe all season long. Below, we cover what to look for in the best sandals for diabetics, how to care for your feet, and when to seek professional guidance.

Why Diabetic Feet Need Special Sandals

Diabetic neuropathy affects up to 50% of people with long-term diabetes. Key concerns include:

Loss of protective sensation: You may not feel a pebble in your sandal or a hot surface.

Poor circulation: Cuts and blisters heal more slowly.

Risk of infection: Even small wounds can progress if undetected.

Foot deformities: Charcot foot, hammertoes or bunions increase pressure points.

Cushioned Footbed• Memory foam or EVA foam molds to your foot, distributing weight evenly• Thick midsole absorbs shock when walking on hard surfaces

Arch and Heel Support• Contoured arch keeps your foot aligned• Deep heel cup stabilizes the heel and reduces sideways rolling

Recommended Materials and Construction

EVA (Ethylene-Vinyl Acetate): Lightweight, shock-absorbing midsole.

PU (Polyurethane): Durable and resilient cushioning.

Genuine Leather: Molds to foot shape; naturally breathable.

Neoprene or Lycra: Soft, stretchable straps that reduce hotspots.

Look for sandals with removable footbeds—you can insert custom orthotics if recommended by your podiatrist.

Styles to Consider

While personal taste matters, these styles often work well for diabetic neuropathy:

Adjustable Sport Sandals: Rugged soles with multiple Velcro straps.

Everyday Slide-Ons: Wide footbed and single strap, easy on/off.

Closed-Toe Sandals: Mesh or leather toe guard for extra protection.

Orthotic-Friendly Sandals: Deep footbed to accept custom inserts.

Tips for Trying and Breaking In New Sandals

Inspect each sandal for seams, rivets or rough edges before putting them on.

Walk around a store or home for at least 10–15 minutes to test comfort.

Gradually increase wear time over a week to allow your feet to adapt.

Always check your feet before and after wearing new sandals for redness, blisters or pressure marks.

If any spot feels hot or painful, stop using the sandal until you can inspect and resolve the issue.

Daily Summer Foot Care Routine

Consistent foot care helps catch problems early:

Wash and Dry Thoroughly– Use mild soap and lukewarm water– Pat feet completely dry, especially between toes

Moisturize Carefully– Apply a diabetes-safe foot cream to tops and soles (avoid between toes)– Prevent cracks without causing fungal growth

Inspect Feet Daily– Use a mirror or ask for help to see the soles– Look for cuts, blisters, redness or swelling

Trim Nails Straight Across– Prevent ingrown toenails– File edges gently

Change Socks and Footwear Daily– Keep feet dry and reduce bacterial build-up

Warning Signs and When to Seek Help

Despite precautions, problems can still arise. Seek immediate medical attention if you notice:

Open sores, ulcers or bleeding wounds

Increasing redness, warmth or swelling

Painful bumps, calluses or ingrown nails

Foul odor or discharge

Speak to Your Doctor

Choosing the best sandals for diabetics and caring for numb feet can greatly reduce summer foot problems. However, nothing replaces professional advice. Always speak to a doctor or podiatrist about serious or life-threatening symptoms. If you have any sudden changes in sensation, unexplained pain, or wounds that won't heal, get medical help right away.
