Here's what to remember about the ending before you start season 3.

Television's most exciting cat and mouse spy thriller, Killing Eve, returns for season 3 on Sunday, April 12 (two weeks earlier than initially planned). In season 2, MI6 operative Eve (Sandra Oh) was operating close-but-not-too-close to master assassin Villanelle (Jodie Comer), which culminated in a twisty plot and wild finale episode. It seems like a million lifetimes ago since we last saw these characters, so we figured it would be as good a time as any to revisit the season 2 finale to both refresh your memory ahead of the new season and remind everyone why this show is so much fun.

Season 2 brought Eve and Villanelle together, and they were working to figure out what, exactly, billionaire tech guy Aaron Peel (Henry Lloyd-Hughes)—who has some plan with a mysterious weapon—was up to. While Eve and Villanelle underwent their secret quasi-Black Ops operation, their superiors—Carolyn (who oversees Eve, from MI6) and Konstantin (Villanelle's handler, from The 12) were operating in the background.

Villanelle ended up as a "guest" of Peel's in the season 2 finale—but in reality, he was keeping her prisoner. When she discovered footage of him killing women (something he had apparently been doing on a serial basis), this put her in serious danger. But viewers had to remember two things: Carolyn (Fiona Shaw) had given a strict order not to kill Peel, and she's also Villanelle. She doesn't care what anyone tells her to do, and she can kill anyone and anything.

With Eve still surveilling the operation, Villanelle drops the safe word, leading Eve to storm the location. When she enters, Peel tries to turn Villanelle into his own killing machine, joining his sick hobby—instead, she turns him to look at a mirror, forcing him to watch as she cuts his throat. While Eve is appropriately frustrated, it doesn't take long for the real plan to be unveiled: Carolyn and MI6 wanted Peel dead all along, despite what they were explicitly saying. She knew that involving Villanelle would eventually send her on a collision course towards assassinating him, and since her official order was "Do not kill," there's no blood on anyone's hands but Villanelle's. Konstantin (Kim Bodnia) confirms to Villanelle that this was the plan when they both went their separate ways.
