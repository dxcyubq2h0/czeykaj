# Stephen Lang Talks Stunt Training, Parkour, and the Epic Fights in 'Avatar: Fire and Ash'

STEPHEN LANG IS returning to Pandora in James Cameron's third installment of the Avatar franchise, Avatar: Fire and Ash. Before the film's wide release on December 19, Lang stopped in to MH HQ in New York City to catch up with the Strong Talk crew, exercise physiologist and strength coach Dr. Pat Davidson and MH fitness director Ebenezer Samuel, C.S.C.S.

Lang—and his portrayal of Colonel Miles Quaritch—have changed significantly since the original 2009 blockbuster hit. His live-action role shifted when his character was killed in the first film, so 2022's sequel Way of Water saw Quaritch's consciousness resurrected in the body of one of the 9.5-foot tall Na'vi. This transformation of his character came with new efforts to capture his performance and its extreme physicality to depict what it would take to survive a place like Pandora.

Lang took the opportunity to share details about the toughest stunt he’s done for the Avatar franchise, the skills he learned to prepare for the films at 73 years old, and what he actually has in common with his villainous character.

When MH last spoke to Lang in 2022, he was focused on a mix of yoga, weight-training and karate to prepare for the role. He's now honing other skills, from parkour to vaulting to kickboxing with James Cameron.

“The world of Pandora is a physically demanding place,” Lang explains. “On this planet, we're always either falling off something or flying to something or submerging ourselves in the water there. So the physical demands on an actor are pretty strenuous and it really helps to be training constantly.”

Lang says filming the underwater fights (specifically in Avatar: The Way of Water) was the craziest stunt he’s done for the series so far. “In a static breath hold, I could do over four and a half minutes. But if you're fighting underwater and the take is going to be a minute, that's a long time to be under,” he explains. “So I found that always very challenging.”

Expect to see another jaw-dropping fight in Avatar: Fire and Ash. Lang reveals there’s a free-for-all battle at the end of the film between he and Sam Worthington’s character, Jake Sully. This time it's above the water in the sky. “We're just all over the place in this particular fight, and that was pretty challenging doing the leaping about that was necessary to do that.”

Lang, Davidson, and Samuel also spoke more in depth about his unique training regimen, why he's embracing fluidity and flexibility over muscle at his age, and how the archetype of the action hero has changed in Hollywood.
