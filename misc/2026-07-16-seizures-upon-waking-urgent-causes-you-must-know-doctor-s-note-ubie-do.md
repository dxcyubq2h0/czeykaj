What causes seizures to occur upon waking up?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Seizures upon waking are often linked to shifts in brain activity that occur during the transition between sleep and wakefulness. Certain types of epilepsy, such as juvenile myoclonic epilepsy and awakening grand mal epilepsy, are especially prone to causing seizures shortly after waking. Sleep deprivation, stress, and irregular sleep schedules can further increase seizure risk during this vulnerable window.

Epilepsy Types: Some types of epilepsy, like 'grand mal on awakening,' are more likely to cause seizures when a person wakes up. This is because the brain's electrical activity changes during sleep and waking periods.

Sleep and Epilepsy: Sleep can influence epilepsy in different ways. The brain goes through various stages during sleep, and some of these stages can make seizures more likely.
