# Science Reveals Why This Man Can Withstand Insanely Cold Temperatures for Hours

[This Man Can](https://neurocurrent.china-akan.com/)

[This Man Can Withstand](https://clinical.peppotom.com/article/protein-and-peptide-drug-delivery-system-m-pharm-my-experience-validating-measurement-methods-for-protein-and-peptide-drug-delivery-system-m-pharm-peptide-share-7bdab9348abe/)

A new study has revealed how one man is able to use a special breathing method to combat the extreme cold for long periods of time.

[Man Can Withstand](https://pepvoice.com/)

Wim Hof, also known as "The Iceman," is known for his various world records around longtime exposure to the cold. He credits his seemingly superhuman abilities to the Wim Hof Method — a technique he created that involves breathing and meditation.

Researchers at Wayne State University were the first to develop a study with Hof to learn more about how his method works, and why it's been so successful at helping him withstand frigid temperatures.

[Man Can](https://metabolic.peppysun.com/article/cagrilintide-and-cagrisema-uk-2026-research-reference-97d1b90391b2/)

According to a press release from the university, the study took place over the course of three days, using fMRI and PET scans. Wayne State University Professors Otto Muzik and Vaibhav Diwadkar explained in the press release that Hof's technique "[allows] him to generate heat that dissipates to lung tissue and warms circulating blood in the pulmonary capillaries."

The study also showed that Hof's technique can have other positive affects on the body, such as helping with mood control and anxiety reduction.

[Man Can](https://clinical.peppotom.com/article/natriuretic-peptide-anp-dosing-need-to-know-information-safety-stacks-dosing-calculator-and-more-072dfb4be0b4/)

"The practice of the Wim Hof Method may lead to tonic changes in autonomous brain mechanisms, a speculation that has implications for managing medical conditions ranging from diseases of the immune system to more intriguingly psychiatric conditions such as mood and anxiety disorders," Diwadkar said.

The university is hoping to begin new studies to evaluate how helpful the method can be when used for mental health purposes as opposed to extreme cold.

"It is not mysterious to imagine that what we practice can change our physiology," Muzik added. "The goal of our research is to ascertain the mechanisms underlying these changes using objective and scientific analyses, and to evaluate their relevance for medicine."
