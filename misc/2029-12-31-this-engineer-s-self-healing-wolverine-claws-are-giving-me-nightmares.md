# This Engineer's 'Self-Healing' Wolverine Claws Are Giving Me Nightmares

[Wolverine Claws](https://immunopeptide.xianfengchem.com/blog/1655992381.html)

If you’re a comic or sci-fi fan, you’ve probably heard of Hacksmith Industries. The extreme DIY crew is justly well-known for their series of real-world interpretations of iconic comic book technology, from Thor’s hammer to Iron Man’s power gauntlet to Thanos’ sword. (And, of course, they’ve made a whole bunch of lightsabers.) With their new video, the crew is going back to basics, with what might seem a comparably simple project: Wolverine’s adamantium claws.

[Engineer's Self-Healing' Wolverine Claws](https://calmvector.dns0663.com/blog/7381117342.html)

[Engineer's Self-Healing' Wolverine](https://allpepay.com/blog/5866148520.html)

As James Hobson, the titular Hacksmith, points out in the video, this is a callback to his very first project, more than a decade ago, when he also made a pair of Wolverine claws. (He also revisited the character, who he called his favorite superhero, to create a set of bone claws.) But none of those projects included the famous mutant’s healing power.

[Self-Healing' Wolverine Claws](https://insulin-pathways.hbsnzj.com/blog/6439961428.html)

For the new project, Hobson and his crew asked whether they could use technology to mimic that nearly magical self-healing ability. They begin by looking at smart alloys—metals that respond to different kinds of external stimulus. To make claws that will bend and deform, but they return to their original shape, they settle on nickel titanium, the same metal used in orthodontic braces. The key feature of nickel titanium is its ability to keep its shape, even after it’s been warped or bent, once heat has been applied.

Once the claws have been cut and polished, they really do look like a pair of adamantium claws. The testing process is Hobson slashing through everyday objects, including a watermelon, aluminum can, and a seasonally appropriate pumpkin. Mission accomplished.
