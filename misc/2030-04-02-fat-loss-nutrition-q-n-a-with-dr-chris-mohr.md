Dr. Chris Mohr contributed to LL Cool J's latest training book and was on the Montel Williams show along with LL. In addition, Dr. Mohr runs successful weight loss programs in Louisville, KY, and is the co-author of a program that will help you set up your daily nutrition, Meal Plans 101

Q: I'd be interested in the optimum calory intake to lose fat (is is BW x 10 to 11)? Once lean, what should the calory intake be to add lean muscle? And what's the calc for maintenance? What's the optimum ratio of PRO/CARB/FAT? Will carb rotation help? If so what ratio's and what high/low carb's cycles are best?

Answer:I typically use 11, but keep in mind that is of course an estimate. Also remember that the difference between those two numbers is small--for someone who is 200 pounds, it's the difference between 200 calories.

While overtime that can make a huge impact, you would quickly know if you are progressing on a set number of calories by monitoring yourself in the mirror, tracking body fat, scale weight, etc. This is also a reason I helped create Meal Plans 101 because rather than being such a rough estimate of caloric needs, it takes into account specific data about you, your training, etc.

Those are all factors that make a big difference on your calorie needs

I wouldn't get too caught up in the very precise numbers, ratios of macronutrients, etc. It can be tedious to do all those calculations by hand and the novelty of doing so will likely wear off rather quickly.

Again, meal plans 101 does all this dirty work for you. Always keep in mind that it's most important to stick to the basics you know about nutrition, like those covered in the fat loss manual of TT.

[Q'n'A with Dr](https://skincare.bairibu.com/tag/estee-lauder-resilience-multi-effect-tri-peptide-eye-creme-942ea824/comparisons/)

You certainly don't need as many carbs on the days you are less active, but the problem I have with drastic carb cycling is again, it's a novelty--not typically something that you can maintain, or are interested in attempting to maintain, forever.

It's great to lose enough body fat to show your hard work--but what's nicer is being able to maintain that. Sticking to the simple nutrition guidelines will allow you to do just that.

Q: I'd like suggestions on a couple of quick & easy post-lift 'meals', when I lift AFTER dinner. I don't like to load up on carbs or calories right before I go to bed, so post-lift I usually have a fat-free yogurt or cottage cheese with one scoop of protein powder. I'm wondering if there might be better choices for muscle growth & recovery PLUS fat loss.

[with Dr](https://hostdefense-journal.escortskhi.com/blog/4028037427.html)

Answer:You need to eat for recovery, whether you're working out first thing in the morning or at night.

[Nutrition Q'n'A with](https://hostdefense-journal.escortskhi.com/blog/7410932771.html)

Yogurt is a great post workout meal; it's not a full solid meal, so it will be more easily digested than solid food options. If you don't eat for recovery, subsequent workouts will suffer because the longer you go without eating carbs, the harder it will be recover.

[Fat Loss Nutrition](https://bioreg.wiki/vyiccuz/)

So use that time to enjoy your carbs. Recovering from a workout will allow you to have better workouts, meaning you can work at a higher intensity, build more muscle, use more calories, etc and ultimately enhance fat loss.
