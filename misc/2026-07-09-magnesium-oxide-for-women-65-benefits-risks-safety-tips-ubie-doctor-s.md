Magnesium Oxide for Women 65+: Benefits, Risks & Safety Tips

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Magnesium oxide is a supplement women 65+ may use for occasional constipation relief and to correct low magnesium levels, potentially supporting muscle function, nerve signaling, heart rhythm, and bone strength. However, it has poor absorption and can cause diarrhea or, in those with reduced kidney function, dangerous magnesium buildup.

Key considerations for safe use include:

Dosing: Aim for about 320 mg total daily magnesium intake; keep supplements under 350 mg/day unless directed by a doctor.

Drug interactions: Magnesium oxide can interfere with antibiotics, bisphosphonates, and thyroid medications—separate doses by several hours.

Who should avoid it: People with kidney disease, bowel obstruction, or certain heart conditions should use caution or avoid it entirely.

Red flag symptoms: Muscle weakness, irregular heartbeat, confusion, or severe diarrhea warrant immediate medical attention.

Alternatives: Food sources (leafy greens, nuts, seeds) or better-absorbed forms like magnesium glycinate or citrate may be preferable.

Because symptoms like fatigue, muscle cramps, constipation, or irregular heartbeat can stem from many causes—not just low magnesium—it's important to understand what's actually driving how you feel before starting any supplement. Magnesium Oxide for Women 65+: Benefits, Risks & Safety Tips

Magnesium is an essential mineral that supports hundreds of processes in the body, including muscle movement, nerve signaling, heart rhythm, and bone health. Magnesium oxide is one of the most commonly used magnesium supplements, especially among older adults. For women age 65 and older, magnesium oxide can be helpful in certain situations—but it also comes with limitations and safety considerations that are important to understand.

This guide explains the benefits, risks, and safe use of magnesium oxide for women 65+, using clear, practical language and information drawn from established medical guidance.

What Is Magnesium Oxide?

Magnesium oxide is a compound made of magnesium and oxygen. It contains a high amount of elemental magnesium, which is why it is commonly used in supplements and some medications.

However, magnesium oxide is not absorbed as efficiently as some other forms of magnesium. Because of this, it is often used for specific purposes rather than general magnesium replacement.

Magnesium oxide is commonly used to:

Treat or prevent magnesium deficiency

Relieve constipation (as an osmotic laxative)

Normal heart rhythm

For women with low magnesium levels, correcting the deficiency may support overall cardiovascular and neurological health. However, magnesium oxide is not a treatment for heart disease.

Risks and Limitations of Magnesium Oxide

While magnesium oxide is widely available, it is not risk-free—especially for older adults.

1. Lower Absorption

Compared with other forms, magnesium oxide has:

Lower bioavailability

Less efficient absorption

This means higher doses may be needed, which increases the chance of side effects.

2. Digestive Side Effects

Common side effects include:

Diarrhea

Bloating

Abdominal discomfort

In older adults, frequent diarrhea can lead to dehydration or mineral imbalances if not addressed.

3. Kidney Health Concerns

Magnesium is cleared from the body by the kidneys. Women 65+ are more likely to have reduced kidney function, sometimes without obvious symptoms.

If the kidneys cannot remove excess magnesium efficiently, levels can rise too high.

Symptoms of excess magnesium may include:

Nausea

Weakness

Low blood pressure

Confusion

Slowed heart rate

This is uncommon but potentially serious and always requires medical attention.

4. Medication Interactions

Magnesium oxide can interfere with the absorption or effectiveness of certain medications, including:

Thyroid medications

Some antibiotics

Osteoporosis medications

Diuretics

Timing and dosage matter, so coordination with a healthcare professional is essential.

Safe Dosage Guidelines for Women 65+

General guidance from established health authorities suggests:

The recommended daily magnesium intake from all sources for older women is about 320 mg per day

Supplements should usually stay below 350 mg per day unless directed by a doctor

Important notes:

Magnesium oxide contains a high amount of elemental magnesium, so dosing should be calculated carefully

More is not better—excess intake increases side effects without improving benefits

Practical Safety Tips

To use magnesium oxide safely:

Start low: Begin with the smallest effective dose

Take with food to reduce stomach upset

Avoid long-term daily use for constipation unless advised by a doctor

Stay hydrated, especially if stools become loose

Space it away from other medications by at least 2 hours

Stop taking magnesium oxide and seek medical advice if you experience persistent diarrhea, weakness, or changes in heart rhythm.
