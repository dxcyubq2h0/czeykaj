What is Cutaneous Squamous Cell Carcinoma (cSCC) in situ (Bowen's Disease)?

[Disease Quiz](https://memorycircuits.hbsnzj.com/blog/9757384105.html)

This is an early, pre-cancerous skin condition that is highly treatable. The affected area is characterized by a slow-growing red or brown scaly patch on the skin that is not painful. It usually occurs in older adults and is associated with factors like long-term exposure to the sun.

Diagnostic Questions for Cutaneous Squamous Cell Carcinoma (cSCC) in situ (Bowen's Disease)

Your doctor may ask these questions to check for this disease:

Are there black, blue, or brown spots on your skin? (Including moles)

Do you have redness on sun-exposed skin?

Do you have dry and flaky skin?

Is your skin rough to touch?

Do you have multiple red areas or spots on your skin?

Treatment of Cutaneous Squamous Cell Carcinoma (cSCC) in situ (Bowen's Disease)

There are various treatments for Bowen's disease. They include applying creams or ointments, phototherapy, scraping or removing the affected part of the skin, or cryotherapy, which uses liquid nitrogen to freeze off the affected skin.
