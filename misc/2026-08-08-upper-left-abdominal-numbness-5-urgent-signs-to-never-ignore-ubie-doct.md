What could lead to numbness in the upper left side of the abdomen?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Numbness in the upper left side of the abdomen is often caused by nerve-related issues. Common culprits include a herniated disc compressing spinal nerves, irritation or entrapment of abdominal wall nerves (such as intercostal or ilioinguinal nerves), and post-surgical nerve damage. Less commonly, conditions like shingles, diabetic neuropathy, or peripheral nerve disorders may also lead to numbness in this area.

Because numbness in the upper left abdomen can stem from many different sources—ranging from mild nerve irritation to conditions that require medical attention—it's important not to guess. Explanation

Numbness in a specific area like the upper left abdomen may happen when nerves do not work correctly. One possibility is irritation or damage to the nerves near the spine due to a herniated disc. For example, when a disc in the thoracic (middle back) area pushes out, it might press on nerves that send signals to the abdominal wall. This can create a feeling of numbness or tingling in that area.

Another cause is a condition called ilioinguinal neuropathy. This occurs when the ilioinguinal nerve, which helps provide feeling in parts of the lower abdomen and groin, becomes irritated or damaged. Although the ilioinguinal nerve is usually linked to the lower area, nerve issues can sometimes be unpredictable and spread to nearby regions.
