Is it safe to take sleeping pills if you have narcolepsy?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Sleeping pills are generally not recommended for people with narcolepsy. Common over-the-counter and prescription sleep aids can interfere with narcolepsy medications such as sodium oxybate (Xyrem) and may worsen daytime sleepiness rather than improve nighttime sleep quality. Narcolepsy is a neurological disorder affecting the brain's ability to regulate sleep-wake cycles, so it requires targeted treatment rather than standard insomnia remedies. Always consult a healthcare provider before combining any sleep aid with narcolepsy therapy.

People with narcolepsy often have trouble staying awake during the day, but taking sleeping pills is not the best solution. Sleeping pills can interfere with narcolepsy treatments, like oxybate, which is used to help manage symptoms.

Why Sleeping Pills May Not Be Safe:

Interference with Treatment: Sleeping pills might affect how well narcolepsy medications work.

Mistiming Risks: Even a single night's mistiming of narcolepsy medication can lead to issues, as noted in medical reports.

Recommended Treatment:

Oxybate: This is a preferred treatment for narcolepsy, as it helps manage symptoms effectively. Clinicians often choose oxybate because it helps improve nighttime sleep, which can reduce daytime sleepiness.
