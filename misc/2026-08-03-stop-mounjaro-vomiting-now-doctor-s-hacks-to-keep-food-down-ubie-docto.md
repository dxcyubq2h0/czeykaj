Mounjaro commonly causes nausea, vomiting, and reduced appetite, especially during dose increases. Physician-approved strategies to ease these side effects include slowing dose titration, switching to evening injections, and eating small, low-fat meals separated from fluid intake to improve tolerance.

Below, you'll find detailed guidance on dietary adjustments, prescription antiemetics like ondansetron, hydration strategies using electrolyte solutions, and critical red-flag symptoms—such as persistent vomiting, signs of dehydration, or severe abdominal pain—that signal when to seek medical care.

3. Dietary and Lifestyle "Nausea Hacks"

Eat Small, Frequent, Low-Fat Meals

Aim for 5–6 mini-meals instead of 3 large ones.

Low-fat foods empty the stomach faster; high-fat delays emptying and worsens nausea.

Separate Solids and Liquids

Drink fluids 30–60 minutes before or after meals, not during.

This prevents stomach overfilling and reduces the nausea trigger.

4. Medical Interventions

Consider Prescription or Over-the-Counter Anti-Emetics

Ondansetron (Zofran) 4–8 mg before meals can block nausea receptors.

Prochlorperazine (Compazine) in low doses may help.

Metoclopramide (Reglan) can speed up gastric emptying—but discuss risks (e.g., tardive dyskinesia).

B-Vitamin Complex

Some evidence suggests vitamin B6 at 25–50 mg three times daily can reduce nausea (commonly used in pregnancy).

Acupressure or Acupuncture

Wrist bands that press on the P6 (Nei Guan) point may provide modest relief.

Review Concomitant Medications

Check if any other drugs you take also cause nausea (e.g., antibiotics, pain meds).

Talk to your doctor about adjusting or spacing them out.
