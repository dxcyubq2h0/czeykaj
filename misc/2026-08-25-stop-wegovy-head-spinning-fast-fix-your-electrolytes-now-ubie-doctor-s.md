Doctor's Note: Head Spinning When You Stand Up on Wegovy? Fix Your Electrolytes

Starting Wegovy (semaglutide) for weight management can bring welcome changes, but some people notice lightheadedness or a spinning sensation when standing up. This isn't uncommon, and often stems from shifts in blood pressure or body electrolytes. Below, we'll explain why "Wegovy and dizziness when standing" can occur, how to spot an electrolyte imbalance, and simple steps to feel steadier on your feet.

What's Happening When You Stand Up?When you rise from sitting or lying down, gravity pulls blood toward your legs. Your body normally responds by tightening blood vessels and boosting heart rate to keep blood flowing to your brain. If that response is delayed or blunted, you get less blood flow briefly, causing:

Lightheadedness

A "head spinning" sensation

Blurry vision

Weakness or feeling faint

This drop in blood pressure upon standing is called orthostatic hypotension. Wegovy itself doesn't directly lower blood pressure, but related effects—like reduced appetite, nausea, vomiting, or dehydration—can contribute.

Electrolytes: The Key to Stable Blood PressureElectrolytes are minerals that carry electrical charges in your body. They regulate fluid balance, nerve signals and muscle contractions. The main players are:

Sodium

Potassium

Magnesium

Calcium

Chloride

If electrolyte levels fall too low—often from dehydration or GI upset—your blood pressure regulation can falter, making dizziness more likely.

How to Fix and Prevent Electrolyte Imbalances on Wegovy

Stay Hydrated• Sip water continuously—aim for at least 8 cups (2 liters) daily unless your doctor advises otherwise.• Include low-sugar electrolyte drinks if you've had significant vomiting or diarrhea.

Replenish Key Minerals• Sodium: Add a pinch of salt to meals or broths.• Potassium: Eat fruits like bananas, oranges, avocados; include spinach, sweet potatoes.• Magnesium: Snack on nuts, seeds, whole grains; consider a supplement if levels are low.• Calcium: Drink low-fat milk or fortified plant milk; eat yogurt, cheese.

Adjust Meal Timing and Size• Smaller, more frequent meals can reduce nausea and improve overall intake.• Don't' skip fluids between or with meals.

Rise Slowly• Before standing, sit at the edge of the bed or chair for 30–60 seconds.• Stand gradually, holding onto something stable until you feel balanced.

Monitor Symptoms• Keep a log of episodes of dizziness, timing, fluid/electrolyte intake, and any GI symptoms.• Share this with your healthcare provider at follow-up visits.

Consider Mineral Supplements• Only under doctor supervision, especially if blood tests show deficiencies.• Over-supplementing can cause its own problems, so follow prescribed doses.

Lifestyle Tips to Support Stable Blood Pressure

Wear compression stockings if you have frequent orthostatic symptoms.

Elevate the head of your bed by 4–6 inches to reduce overnight fluid pooling in legs.

Limit caffeine and alcohol, which can dehydrate you.

Engage in light leg exercises (ankle pumps) if you're sitting for long periods.

When to Seek Medical HelpMost mild dizziness improves with fluid and electrolyte adjustments. However, call your doctor or seek urgent care if you experience:

Fainting (loss of consciousness)

Chest pain, shortness of breath, or severe palpitations

Confusion, severe headache, slurred speech or vision changes

Persistent vomiting or diarrhea beyond 24 hours
