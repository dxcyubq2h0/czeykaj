Go slower when you lower You can trigger greater muscle growth when you focus on the lowering—or eccentric—portion of the curl. The reason: Gravity is assisting you on the way down, so you can use more weight, says Gaddour.

Your fibers are being forcibly lengthened during the eccentric, which causes the most muscle damage, too. As your body repairs the fibers, they make them stronger and thicker, leading to bigger guns in the process, he explains.

Do it: Select a load that’s 10 to 20 percent heavier than you can normally curl for 5 reps. (You may have to use momentum to propel the weights up to the top of the curl.) Slowly lower the weights, taking at least 5 seconds. That’s 1 rep.

Perform 5 total reps and then rest for 2 minutes. Repeat for a total of 3 to 5 sets.

Pause for a more strength When you lower, you resist gravity. When you lift, you fight against gravity. And when you perform an isometric hold, you meet gravity in the middle to exert the same amount of force.

Pausing during the biceps curl at the most difficult part—when your elbow is bent at a 90-degree angle—increases the time your muscles are under tension at their weakest point. Even a light weight will feel challenging, says Gaddour.
