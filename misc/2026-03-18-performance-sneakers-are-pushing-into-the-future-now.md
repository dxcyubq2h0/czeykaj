# Performance Sneakers Are Pushing Into the Future, Now

Our product picks are editor-tested, expert-approved. We may earn a commission through links on our site. Why Trust Us?

Ever since the 1980s, when the Air Max sole finally brought some much-needed cushion to sneakers, Nike’s mission has been simple: find what’s wrong and fix it. “Our intention,” says Kathy Gomez, Nike’s VP of NXT Footwear, “is to solve problems for athletes.”

Nearly four decades later, Nike is still on that same mission to solve the problems in sneakers. But today (and in the next decade) that means pushing way beyond Air. As it continues to optimize both sneaker fit and function, the company is forcing you to rethink everything you've come to expect from your kicks.

And it's doing more than that too: As it's FY20 Impact report shows, it's working to promote and drive diversity and inclusion as much as possible, both in the community and behind closed doors. In 2020, Nike increased its representation of women globally to 49.5%, and 29% of racial and ethnic minorities at the Vice President levels in the U.S. were racial and ethnic minorities. The sneaker giant also invested $89.8 million in community impact projects, and Nike, Converse, Jordan Brand and Michael Jordan have committed $140 million over the next 10 years to support organizations that empower Black Americans.

Meanwhile, the company continues to evolve its sneakers. Just how fast can your footwear make you go? Do you really need laces (more on all that soon)? And can your sneakers be good for the environment? Yes! Need an example? Since 2015, Nike's 47 million (yes, you read that right) kilograms of manufacturing scraps have been recycled into new footwear products, and energy consumption is down 10% per pair of kicks produced by Nike footwear suppliers.

It's all a forward-thinking approach that keeps yielding sneaker innovations that the environment, your feet, and your on-field performance will love. Here are a few ways Nike is pushing sneakers in the future right now:

Nike’s FlyEase line rethinks laces by adding alternative tightening features. Problem 2: Energy Return

Whenever your foot hits the ground during a run, some of the energy you create dissipates through your shoe into the earth. Some of it is returned to you, propelling you forward—and the more energy that’s returned to you, the faster you’ll go.

Five years ago, Nike debuted Adapt, a self-lacing system right out of Back to the Future II. Adapt tech landed in hoops kicks in 2019, tightening and loosening based on in-game intensity. Nike won’t say what’s next, but we would not be surprised to see a hardcore distance shoe in 2021.
