Explanation

Sharp clavicle pain can be alarming. The clavicle — commonly called the collarbone — connects your breastbone (sternum) to your shoulder blade. It helps stabilize your shoulder and allows a wide range of arm movement. Because the clavicle sits just under the skin and plays a key structural role, it's vulnerable to injury and inflammation.

If your clavicle aches, feels sharp with movement, or is tender to the touch, there are several possible causes. Some are minor and improve with rest. Others require medical evaluation. Here's what you need to know.

Common Causes of Clavicle Pain

1. Clavicle Fracture (Broken Collarbone)

A fractured clavicle is one of the most common shoulder injuries. It often happens due to:

Falls onto the shoulder

Sports injuries

Car accidents

Direct trauma to the collarbone

Symptoms may include:

Sharp, intense pain at the clavicle

Clavicle fractures need medical evaluation. Many heal with a sling and rest, but some require surgery, especially if the bone is displaced.

2. Acromioclavicular (AC) Joint Injury

The AC joint sits where the clavicle meets the shoulder blade. Injury here is sometimes called a "separated shoulder."

Common signs:

Pain at the outer end of the clavicle

Swelling at the top of the shoulder

Pain when lifting the arm

Tenderness directly over the joint

AC joint injuries often occur during contact sports or falls. Mild cases improve with rest, ice, and anti-inflammatory medications. Severe separations may require orthopedic care.

3. Sternoclavicular (SC) Joint Problems

The sternoclavicular joint connects the clavicle to the breastbone. Though less common, injuries or inflammation here can cause sharp pain near the center of the chest.

You may notice:

Pain where the clavicle meets the sternum

Swelling or redness

Pain with deep breathing or arm movement

SC joint injuries should be evaluated because important blood vessels and structures lie behind this joint.

5. Muscle Strain

Muscles that attach near the clavicle include the sternocleidomastoid, pectoral muscles, and deltoid. Overuse, poor posture, or heavy lifting can strain these muscles.

You may feel:

Dull or sharp pain near the collarbone

Tightness in the neck or chest

Pain that worsens with certain movements

Improvement with rest

Muscle strains typically improve within days to weeks with:

Rest

Ice or heat

Gentle stretching

Anti-inflammatory medications (if safe for you)

6. Arthritis

Arthritis can affect both the AC joint and the sternoclavicular joint.

Over time, cartilage wears down, leading to:

Gradual onset of clavicle pain

Stiffness

Clicking or grinding

Worsening discomfort with activity

Osteoarthritis is more common with aging or after prior injury. Rheumatoid arthritis or other inflammatory conditions can also affect the clavicle joints.

7. Infection (Rare but Serious)

Infections of the clavicle bone (osteomyelitis) or nearby joints are uncommon but serious.

Warning signs include:

Fever

Redness and warmth over the clavicle

Severe pain

Swelling

Feeling generally unwell

This requires urgent medical care.

8. Referred Pain

Sometimes pain felt near the clavicle actually originates elsewhere, such as:

Neck problems (cervical spine issues)

Nerve compression

Lung conditions

Gallbladder problems (typically right-sided pain)

If clavicle pain is unexplained or persistent, your doctor may evaluate nearby structures.

When Is Clavicle Pain an Emergency?

Most clavicle pain is not life-threatening. However, seek urgent medical care if you experience:

Chest pressure or tightness

Shortness of breath

Pain spreading to the jaw or arm

Sudden severe pain after trauma

Visible deformity of the collarbone

Numbness or tingling in the arm

Fever with swelling and redness

These symptoms could signal a fracture, infection, or a heart-related issue.

Never ignore chest-related symptoms — it's always safer to get checked.

How Doctors Diagnose Clavicle Pain

Your doctor will typically:

Ask about recent injuries

Review your medical history

Perform a physical exam

Check range of motion

Press along the clavicle and joints

Imaging tests may include:

X-rays to look for fractures or arthritis

CT scans for detailed bone imaging

MRI if soft tissue injury is suspected

Blood tests if infection or inflammatory disease is possible

Clear diagnosis guides proper treatment.

Treatment depends on the cause. Options may include:

Anti-inflammatory medications (if appropriate)

Physical therapy

Can You Prevent Clavicle Pain?

While not all causes are preventable, you can reduce risk by:

Wearing protective gear during sports

Strengthening shoulder and upper back muscles

Practicing good posture

Avoiding repetitive overuse

Addressing minor injuries early

Healthy bones also depend on:

Adequate calcium and vitamin D

Weight-bearing exercise

Avoiding smoking

The Bottom Line

Sharp clavicle pain can stem from many causes — from simple muscle strain to fractures or joint inflammation. The clavicle plays a crucial role in shoulder stability, so pain in this area deserves attention.

Most cases improve with conservative treatment. However, sudden injury, deformity, fever, or chest symptoms require immediate evaluation.
