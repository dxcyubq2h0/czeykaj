Cody Rigsby is a different kind of fitness instructor. While other Peloton instructors provide the type of rah-rah you expect from a cycling instructor combined with a healthy dose of self-help “you are worthy” talk, the 34-year old Rigsby tells riders things like “if Britney can get through 2007, you can get up this hill.”

He’s funny, he makes rides fun, and it’s made him the at-home spin bike’s biggest star: Rigsby has more than a million followers on Instagram, has appeared as a contestant on Dancing with the Stars, and his pithy lines—including that one about Britney—have spawned a cottage industry of Etsy stores hawking mugs featuring his quotes. And in his “XOXO Cody” rides, Rigsby dispenses with the normal cycling format all together to answer rider questions.

But despite his success in carving out his own niche as the fun, talkative instructor, Rigsby says that’s not his preference for his own workouts.

“I go for the opposite of who I am,” he says. “Maybe I'm really verbose and really have a ton of personality and make my workouts fun, or silly. Whereas I like someone who's going to tell me exactly what to do, push me past my limit, and kind of just be really straightforward with my workout.”

Right now, Rigsby making a push to add more muscle to his frame. He spoke to Men's Health about how he’s eating more calories to add on that lean tissue, what he does on leg day, and how meditation is helping him with recovery.

This interview has been condensed and edited for clarity.

Whether it’s after a hard ride or a strength workout, what’s a go-to shake you like for recovery?

[Cody Rigsby Gain Muscle](https://thymosin-report.hbsnzj.com/)

I’m trying to kind of put on a little bit of muscle now, and I've always found that an easy way to kind of put calories into my diet to put on a little bit more weight, more muscle mass, is oats. So I put a full cup of oats into a shake, and mine's kind of easy: Like I usually do oat milk, a cup of oats and just whey and maybe a container of raspberries.

What’s your strength plan like for packing on that mass?

Obviously I'm doing my cardio with Peloton. [For strength], I have a trainer, and we're kind of working body part-specific, bodybuilder-type workouts. It's a chest day, back and triceps day, shoulders and triceps day, and a leg day.

I have a love-hate relationship with leg day. It's the hardest, it's the most challenging, but I feel the best after. And I like I feel that my legs are my strongest things. So it's always like the hardest thing to train, you know? My legs have become so conditioned from Peloton.

And then, you know, I travel a lot. When I travel, it's hard for me to find motivation and that's when I always use the Peloton app. I love the 30-minute strength classes because it makes me feel like I'm staying within my schedule.

Love it or hate it, what do you tend to do on that leg day?

[Peloton Star Cody](https://cellular.wiki/jx0j8757/)

It really depends. But we're definitely running squats. Deadlift. Leg press. Hamstring curls. Usually in a circuit style. This is why I love having a trainer because they just think of it and do it for me, where I'm just showing up and doing the work. It's usually a circuit of 2 to 3 exercises, 12 to 15 reps, with a 2 to 3 minute recovery in between each.

You started with Peloton in your 20s. You’re now in your mid-30s. What’s changed for you, training-wise, over that time?

As a younger man, I think it's easy to get to jump into workouts, not worry about stretching, not worrying about anything, just kind of doing the work. As I've gotten older, I think it's been important for me to really discover and create a relationship with myself physically, mentally, emotionally. And what has helped is really creating that relationship with myself through meditation. And that in itself builds a mental and physical awareness. That helps me be aware of how I need to treat my body—if that's taking a rest day, maybe when my ego is telling me that I should be working out that day, but my body's not.

It's good to listen to my body and allow myself a rest day or active recovery days where it's focusing on stretching, focusing on mobility and focusing on just being grateful for what my body can do. And so as I've gotten older, I think it's just important to create that relationship with myself and give myself a degree of of recovery and taking it easy and also really understanding that taking that time off is going to make me stronger and more efficient in my workouts, and that I can really bounce back and hit it harder if I can just take the time off.

For a lot of high-energy guys, like yourself, meditation can be tough at first—it’s hard to turn the mind off. Did you have trouble at first?

It was so, so challenging. A big reason that I got into meditation was I was going through a really hard breakup. So I was constantly thinking about that breakup. And the first time I decided to meditate, it was really challenging. Because your brain is never going to turn off. It's never going to be quiet. The thing that we do in meditation is we start to be more present and start to recognize that thoughts are going to happen. But we don't have to get attached to every thought, and we can kind of just let them go by. So it's just kind of labeling things for what they are instead of letting them affect you constantly. Because if you start to get attached to a thought, then a lot of feelings come out of that. And sometimes, you know, we just need to let all of that pass. But it took me a really long time to get to that place.

And meditation is just like working out at first. It's going to feel crazy. At first you're going to feel dumb, you're going to feel like you can't do it. And the more that you're in an uncomfortable space and the more that you allow yourself to figure out, it becomes second nature, and it becomes a habitual part of your wellness routine.

We talked about what you’ve added to your life over that time, with meditation and rest days. Is there anything you’ve taken out that you think other guys in your age range should consider subtracting?

This one is the easiest answer, but it's also the hardest for people to do: It's alcohol. They should subtract alcohol as much as possible. As I get older, hangovers are worse and worse, and they come easier and easier. I can drink like two or three drinks and still be kind of hung over, sluggish the next day. And, you know, even this year, I have really created a relationship and an awareness of my relationship with alcohol. I never have seen myself having a problem with it. I probably drink like once or twice a week, but that is becoming less and less just because I know that it affects me more and I highly prioritize sleep—and alcohol messes with my sleep. And then I prioritize my ability to be efficient and work out hard. So if I had drinks the day before, I know that it's hard for me to perform the next day. So I have been avoiding it and understanding that I don't need it as much.

You talk about your mom, Cindy, a lot in your rides. Does she take your classes on the bike and see that? What has been her reaction to your classes, and including stories about her?

I got her a bike a while ago, and, you know, she's going to be 68 this year. She has some mental health issues. She's diabetic. And she's just, like, not as well connected to technology as myself or people of my generation. So, times I got her on the bike, she's just like mesmerized by the fact that I'm on there. And I think what she loves to see is the silliness and the fun. And I think she gets a kick out of that. And, you know, I think I credit a lot of my silliness and my playfulness to her. She's always been a source of fun to me. And also, she can kind of be brash and inappropriate at times, and I think that's also where I get it from. So when she sees that on the bike or when she watched me on Dancing with the Stars, I think it really makes her proud that she sees her influence in how I approach life.

I know you’ll have a great one for this: What’s your go-to cheat meal?

I would rather eat food than drink alcohol because I love, love, love, love, love, food. My favorite thing, almost too much sometimes. But I allow myself to eat what I want to eat.

There's a restaurant called Pies and Thighs, it's sort of like a southern restaurant, and they have the best chicken biscuit. It's like a chicken fillet with hot sauce and honey and a biscuit. It's the best biscuit—I'm from the South, and I could never find a good biscuit in New York. And it definitely takes the cake. I take it and extra step by adding mayonnaise to it because I'm truly from the South. So that is my favorite go-to cheat meal.
