The researchers crunched data on outcomes from over 32,000 patients who underwent surgical procedures at seven health systems. They discovered that the patients were more likely to experience post-op problems if the surgeon who operated on them garnered more patient or family complaints.

In fact, patients cared for by surgeons who received the most unsolicited complaints experienced 14 percent more surgical and medical complications—say, wound infections, pneumonia, pulmonary embolism, stroke, or sepsis—than those treated by the least disrespectful surgeons, according to the press release.

If those numbers held true throughout all surgical procedures performed across the U.S., that would translate to 350,000 additional post-op complications, study author Gerald Hickson, M.D., said in the statement.

Here’s how your jerk doctor might be putting your health at risk: If a surgeon behaves rudely, disrespectfully, or dismissively to other medical pros—like nurses or anesthesiologists—they may be less likely to speak up if they notice changes in your condition, the researchers say.

Bottom line? If you feel like your doctor is being rude or disrespectful, report it to the hospital. Previous research has shown that sharing complaints with doctors can result in better behavior and fewer malpractice claims, the authors told STAT.
