# Dwyane Wade Just Made a Powerful Statement About Being a Dad to an LGBTQ+ Child

Former pro basketball player Dwyane Wade just gave an illuminating interview with the All the Smoke show about parenting an LGBTQ+ child, and how being there for his 12-year-old, Zion, has involved challenging his own preconceived notions of what it means to be a good dad.

"I had to look myself in the mirror and say, 'What if your son come home and tells you he's gay? What are you going to do? How are you going to be?'" He said, recalling the first time he and his wife Gabrielle Union noticed that Zion wasn't on the same "boy vibe" as his older son, Zaire.

"You can learn something from your kids," he said. "In our household, that's all we talk about. We talk about making sure our kids be seen by each of us. We want them to be whoever they feel that they can be in this world, that's our goal."

He also had to acknowledge that raising a child who was both black and a member of the LGBTQ+ community meant that their family would face criticism from multiple sides. "While you trying to go down that process, this is what's going to come at you," he said. "It's going to be a lot of negativity, it's going to be a lot of hate, and it's not even just from my son's sexuality, it's just about being a young black man, or a black woman, right?"

Wade has publicly responded to some of the people who have felt the need to voice an opinion about how he and his partner are raising their children, such as the time they shared a family photo in which Zion was wearing a crop top and manicured nails.

[Dad to an LGBTQ](https://ppmaas.com/b42nbw3s0/)

"When I respond to things socially, I'm not responding because you're hurting my feelings," he said. "Why I'm responding is because I understand my platform. I understand that I'm speaking for a lot of people that don't have the same voice that I have... It ain't about him, he knows who he is. It's about you, who are you? All these people out here saying those things, look at yourself. Understand that you the one that got the issues. You the one that got the problems. It's not the kids."

Wade also stressed the importance of parents actively educating themselves on the issues that their kids are going to face as they go through life, in order to be able to offer the kind of love and support that they will need. With the advocacy group The Trevor Project citing rejection by close family as one of the many factors that lead to the high rates of homelessness and suicide among LGBTQ+ youth in the United States, the importance of this kind of positive and proactive messaging to parents cannot be overstated.

"I've watched my son from day one become into who she now eventually come into," Wade said. "Nothing changes with my love. Nothing changes with my responsibilities. Only thing I've got to do now is get smarter and educate myself more."
