# The Greatest Olympic Moments of All Time

The Tokyo 2020 Summer Olympic Games may be over, but we're still talking about the highlights. That's because the competitions are bigger than the 16 days they dominate screens. The Olympics are what makes sports history — and because they're so widely-watched, the big moments stick in the minds of everyone. We're taking a look back at just some of the great moments through the decades. From record-shattering wins to extreme acts of perseverance, these are what we consider the greatest Olympic moments of all time.

1924: Charles Jewtraw Makes History for America

America scored big at the very first Winter Olympic Games in 1924, after Charles Jewtraw took home the gold in Chamonix, France. The speed skater's victory had a huge impact on the sport in the country, as he was the first American to win a gold medal during the Winter Olympics.

1936: Jesse Owens Shatters Records in Berlin, Germany

American track and field star Jesse Owens was the bright spot of the otherwise controversial 1936 Summer Olympics in Berlin, Germany. The athlete won four gold medals and broke three world records during the competitions, becoming a symbol of Olympic glory in the United States.

1948: Dick Button Lands a Gold in Figure Skating

More than twenty years after the United States' first Winter Olympic gold medal, Dick Button managed to cinch the prize in the popular sport of figure skating. During the 1948 Winter Olympic Games in St. Moritz, Switzerland, Dick Button became the first American to win a gold medal in figure skating.

1948: Diver Victoria Manalo Draves Wins Gold Medal in London

Victoria Manalo Draves won two gold medals at the 1948 Summer Olympics in London, England. Her victories in both platform and springboard diving were not only a big win for the U.S., but a milestone for all Asian-Americans as well, as she became the first Asian-American athlete to win an Olympic gold medal.

1952: Emil Zátopek Wins Gold During His First-Ever Marathon

Czechoslovakian long distance runner Emil Zátopek is remembered for his record-breaking wins during the Helsinki 1952 Olympics Games. His first two medals were won during the 5000m and 10000m races, while his third was won when he decided to enter the Olympic marathon event — his very first ever.

1960: Cassius Clay Enters the World Stage

It was during the 1960 Olympics in Rome, Italy, that a young Muhammad Ali made his big debut on the world stage. The boxer was still fighting under his given name, Cassius Clay, when he won a gold medal in his fight against Zbigniew Pietrzykowski. Just one month after the Olympics, the boxer went pro and became the heavyweight champion known all over the world.

1960: Wilma Rudolph Dominates in Track and Field

Wilma Rudolph took home multiple medals at the 1960 Summer Games in Rome, Italy. After competing in the 1956 Olympics, she returned four years later and made history as the first American woman to win three gold medals in track and field at a single Olympics.

1960: Abebe Bikila Wins the Olympic Marathon in Rome

Abebe Bikila, an Ethiopian runner, completed the 1960 Olympic marathon barefoot. The best part? He took home the gold medal.

1968: Bob Beamon Breaks Olympic Record on the Long Jump

While competing in the 1968 Olympic Games in Mexico City, American track and field athlete Bob Beamon shocked fans with an impressive record-breaking performance. Not only did Beamon win the gold medal in the event, but he also shattered the long jump record by two feet. The record remained unbroken until Mike Powell in 1991.

1968: American Sprinters Protest from the Podium

When American sprinters Tommie Smith and John Carlos earned the gold and bronze Olympic medals in 1968, they saw an opportunity to take a stand. The athletes threw their fists into the air, giving the Black Power salute from the podium in protest of racial injustice in the United States.

1972: Mark Spitz Wins the Most Olympic Medals of All Time

Mark Spitz made the history books at the 1972 Munich Summer Olympics as one of the greatest swimmers of all time. The American athlete took home a total of seven gold medals and shattered several world records for his finishing time.

1972: Olga Korbut Earns Gold with a Dangerous Move

The Soviet Union had an impressive year at the 1972 Olympics in Munich, Germany, thanks to star athlete Olga Korbut. The gymnast won three gold medals and successfully completed the the “Korbut Flip," which is now banned from competitions.

1976: Nadia Comaneci Nails a Perfect Score

Romanian gymnast Nadia Comaneci's 1976 Olympic performance turned out to be a milestone moment for the sport of gymnastics. Comaneci earned the first perfect score in the history of the Olympic sport for her uneven bar routine in Montreal, Canada.

1980: The Miracle on Ice

During the peak of the Cold War, the United States faced off against the Soviet Union in the Lake Placid 1980 Winter Olympics. The USA men's ice hockey team managed to secure a 4-3 victory over the Soviet Union during the semi-final competition. The match — dubbed "the Miracle on Ice" —was a symbol of national pride, and the United States went on to win the gold medal.

1984: Mary Lou Retton Wins Big for the United States

After decades of Olympic gymnastics dominated by Eastern European nations, Mary Lou Retton took home a gold medal at the Los Angeles 1984 Summer Games and became the first American, male or female, to do so.

1984: Jayne Torvill and Christopher Dean's Perfect Routine

Jayne Torvill and Christopher Dean earned the gold medal and perfect scores from every judge for their performance of Ravel’s “Bolero.”

1984: Carl Lewis' Record-Breaking Sprint

American athlete Carl Lewis first competed in the Olympics in 1984, where he took home four gold medals. Additionally, he set a world record in the 100m sprint, beating his closest opponent by 8 feet.

1988: Greg Louganis Wins Gold After Horrible Injury

Fans took note of Greg Louganis after the American diver struck his head on the diving board during preliminary rounds in Seoul, South Korea. Despite needing stitches in his head, Louganis overcame his injury to win a gold medal for the United States.

1988: Florence Griffith Joyner Becomes the Fastest Woman in the World

Thanks to a few record-breaking performances during the 1988 Summer Games, Florence Griffith Joyner made Olympic history. The American track and field star earned four medals, three of which were gold, and became known as the fastest woman in the world.

1988: The Jamaican Bobsleigh Team

The first time Jamaica was represented in bobsleigh was in 1988, and boy, did it garner attention. The team was so beloved, it didn’t even matter that the group missed out on winning a medal in Calgary, Canada.
