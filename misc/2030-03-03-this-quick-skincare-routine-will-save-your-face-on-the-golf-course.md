I TRY TO sneak in a few rounds of golf per month with my crew. One thing I almost never see in the parking lot or on the first tee? Guys putting on sunscreen. Americans played a record 545 million rounds in 2024—more than 10 percent higher than pre-pandemic levels. That’s a lot of faces, ears, and scalps baking out there. Spend five hours in direct sun without sunscreen during peak sun time, and you’re fast-tracking both skin cancer and older, wrinkly skin.

“I cut skin cancers off golfers all day,” says Whitney Hovenic, MD, a board-certified dermatologist in Reno, Nevada. “Guys wear hats and think they’re covered, but the sides and tops of the ears get wrecked.” Just look at Stewart Cink’s famous hat tan—he’s a legendary golfer, but he’s the most brutal PSA for sunscreen you’ll ever see.

The chronic sun exposure isn’t just about cancer risk, either. Terrance Keaney, MD, a board-certified dermatologist based in Washington, D.C., says golfers and boaters are the two groups he sees with the highest rates of sun damage. “It’s sun spots, redness, and that leathered skin you see after years of exposure,” says Dr. Keaney.

At 43 years old, I like to think I look younger than most of the guys on the course. For that, I credit SPF and a solid pre-game routine. I would rather spend a minute with sunscreen in the parking lot than hours in a dermatologist’s office paying for laser treatments to undo years of damage. Save your money and your face with a routine that prevents the problem. If you’re hitting the links, here’s how to keep your skin safe for 18 holes:

Step 1: Clean Your Face

Think of your skin like the grip on your clubs. If it’s dry, cracked, or unprotected, you’re already starting at a disadvantage. Before you head to the course, start with a gentle cleanser to wash off sweat, oil, and last night’s grime. Your skin should feel clean, not tight.

The formula mixes niacinamide to refine pores and even skin tone, aloe and centella to calm irritation, and hyaluronic acid to lock in hydration. That combo makes it solid for guys who wash their face multiple times per day. Plus, the founder built Strive specifically for men, and you can tell it works well without overcomplicating things.

Step 2: Add a Lightweight Moisturizer

Use a lightweight moisturizer with hydrating ingredients like hyaluronic acid or squalene. Hydrated skin is less likely to get irritated by the sun, wind, or the sunscreen itself.

Step 3: Apply Sunscreen as Close to Your First Tee Shot as Possible

Apply broad-spectrum SPF 30+ to your face (especially the crease by your glasses), ears, lips, and neck before you leave the parking lot. Guys are historically terrible at this—we often rub SPF into our hands first (wasting half the sunscreen), and forget these important spots on our body. Look for something sweat and water-resistant, but honestly, just pick the one you’ll actually use.
