The Back Specialization Workout

Mobility Warmup: Do a lat hang for 5 to 10 deep belly breaths.

1. Cable Rope Face Pull With External Rotation Bias

This move is designed to strengthen your rotator cuff, build your upper and mid-back, and bulletproof your shoulders.

The pull pattern is a bit of a “tweener” between a row (horizontal pull) and pullup (vertical pull), so it hits your back muscles from a totally new angle to spur new growth.

This is also a great warmup for the more challenging moves to come.

2. Cable Rope Face Pull With Internal Rotation Bias

This exercise is identical to the one above, but with an internal rotation bias (overhand grip).

Do 2 to 3 sets of 15 to 20 reps. Rest a minute between sets.

3. Dumbbell Chest-Supported Row

The chest-supported rowing option is a better option for most guys when compared to bent-over rowing. It’s easy on your spine (and thus easier to recover from) and really forces you to isolate the pulling motion around your shoulder blades.

It’s all good if you have to use a lighter weight than normal in the beginning. You’ll reap more gains in the long run that way.

When you do a rep, hold the top position for 1 second and take 3 seconds to lower the weight.

Perform 3 to 5 sets of 10 to 15 reps. Rest 1 to 2 minutes between sets.

4. Straight-Arm Lat Pulldown

This isolation move focuses on shoulder extension for maximum lat activation.

I recommend leaning forward at the top of the move to maximize the stretch and leaning back at the bottom of the move to maximize the contraction.

A slight bend in your elbows is okay, but I prefer you do these with your arms as straight as possible, no matter how light of a load you need to use. Just focus on “feeling” your lats as much as possible here.

When you do a rep, hold the bottom position for 1 second and take 3 seconds to raise the weight.

Perform 3 to 5 sets of 10 to 15 reps. Rest 1 minute between sets.

5. Trap Bar Deadlift

The latest research shows that you can build more strength, power, and muscle mass with trap bar deads compared to traditional straight-bar deadlifts.

Plus, this move is a lot easier to perform for guys with poor mobility. And since the load is directly under your center of gravity, there is less shearing forces on your spine.

Treat each rep like its own set. Pick the bar up, put it down as quietly as possible, and then pause for a full count. That’s 1 rep. Wash and repeat.

Remember, it’s a DEADlift—you should be lifting a dead weight, so don’t use any bouncing at the bottom of the exercise. This prevents injury and builds more raw strength.

Lower the weight slowly and with control so you can set the weight down as quietly as possible.

Perform 3 to 5 sets of 3 to 5 reps. Rest 2 to 3 minutes between sets.

6. Eccentric Neutral-Grip Pullup

There’s no better back exercise than a pullup. Beyond working nearly every muscle in your upper body and core, it also decompresses your spine—perfect for following up heavy deads—and lengthens out your whole body.

The secret sauce to maxing out your muscle growth potential is to use a slow lowering or eccentric tempo. Take at least 5 seconds to lower from the top of the pullup to a dead hang before you pull yourself back up.
