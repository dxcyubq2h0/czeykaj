# To Spray or Not to Spray

Still pale like me? Another route to a golden bronze is a spray tan. I was skeptical of this method, thinking of cheesy LA starlet wannabes, until I tried it a few weeks before a trip to Italy. I can hide my skim milk colored legs under jeans in New York without a second glance, but there was no way I could cover my pale limbs in the warm temperatures of the Mediterranean.

[or Not to Spray](https://cortexcompass.jcesqc8.com/blog/3353019518.html)

I researched airbrush tanning and stopped into Sundara in New York to check it out. Sundara stood out because it's one of the rare places that actually had a range of colors to choose from – most spray tanning salons use a one-size-fits-all approach, giving you an option of Light, Medium, Medium-Dark and Dark. That’s no way for it to look natural.

I stripped to my skivvies (you can go entirely bare if you want, but I’m kind of modest) and Natalie Cupid answered my questions as she expertly applied with what looked like airbrush paint to my lower half. I grilled her about spray tanning -- and for men specifically, and this is what I learned.

If you need to look tan for an event, I’d suggest doing a test run at least two weeks in advance of the occasion so you don’t show up looking like Valentino on your wedding day.

Similar to over-the-counter tanning cream, make sure you’ve showered, exfoliated are not wearing any moisturizers, deodorant or cologne before you get a spray tan.

Dab moisturizer or Vaseline on “bony” areas - knuckles, knees, elbows, around the hairline, etc. These areas tend to absorb more self-tanner and you should avoid applying the product directly to these areas.

For the most natural results, start with a lighter level and you can always go darker next time.

Wait two weeks between sprays, or until all of the leftover tan is completely gone. I had some leftover self tanner still on my legs and it created darker blotches. Natalie assured me that a good scrubbing before a spray tan gives her an even, smooth canvas to work on.

If possible, wear loose, dark clothing after your spray tanner dries. Do not get wet. Do not shower for at least 12 hours.

[To Spray or Not](https://defensecascade.china-akan.com/blog/1198880840.html)

[Not to](https://insulin-pathways.hbsnzj.com/blog/9854422507.html)

After your spray, don’t take long, hot showers or baths or the tanner will fade more quickly. Get in, get out and do not rub vigorously when you dry off or you'll end up with some rust coloring on those nice, white towels. Pat dry.

Use a light moisturizer each day to keep the color looking smooth – otherwise it can get flaky and weird looking. But do not use any exfoliating moisturizers, such as creams with alpha-hydroxy acids, which will slough off dead skin and make your tan blotchy.

According to Natalie, hairy areas such as chests and legs do not generally cause issues, though some products will generate spots that accumulate at the hair follicle - this is not an issue related to application, it is more related to the product.

Your face will fade faster than other areas of the body, so don’t scrub your face after application – a quick rinse will do. Apply a spray (or any other self-tanning solution) to a clean-shaven face, since shaving accelerates fading by scraping off the skin that pigment is clinging to.

I know it's a lot to digest, but if you are feeling desperate for a quick tan, rather than frying your face in the sun (which only gives you wrinkles, age spots and makes you a high-blood-pressure red color), give this option a whirl.

Good luck, pale face!
