What are the symptoms of Restless Legs Syndrome in older adults?

Internal Medicine

Restless Legs Syndrome (RLS) in older adults is a neurological condition that causes uncomfortable sensations in the legs—often described as tingling, crawling, itching, or aching—typically worsening at rest or at night. Movement, such as walking or stretching, provides temporary relief. RLS can disrupt sleep, reduce quality of life, and may be linked to iron deficiency, nerve issues, or certain medications common in seniors.

Explanation

Restless Legs Syndrome (RLS) in older adults can cause uncomfortable sensations in the legs. These feelings are often described as tingling, crawling, itching, or a burning sensation. The symptoms usually happen when a person is resting or lying down, especially in the evening or at night.

Urge to move: People often feel a strong need to move their legs to relieve these feelings.

Worse at night: Symptoms usually get worse in the evening or at night.
