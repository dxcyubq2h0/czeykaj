# 'Jurassic Park' Is Really a Movie About Running and This Netflix Video Proves It

You might think the premise of the Jurassic Park movies seems fairly straightforward: What if we developed the technology to clone dinosaurs—uh, thanks to something involving mosquitoes preserved in amber—and stuffed them into a deadly amusement park attraction?

But if you ask Netflix, where the original trilogy is now available to stream for the next two months, Jurassic Park is actually about something else entirely: Running. In a new YouTube video posted to the Netflix Film Club fittingly titled Jurassic Park, But Only The Running, Netflix brought the receipts—cutting together a roughly 2-minute video of the franchise's heroes (and occasionally its villains, in the case of Wayne Knight) fleeing all manner of T-Rexes, pterodactyls, a spinosaurus, and of course, the velociraptors—before Chris Pratt managed to tame them.

"Forget all the science, dinosaurs, and plot points. Running is what the Jurassic Park series is really about," Netflix wrote in the video's caption. "Let's just hope they spared no expense on their shoes."

Just in case the clip makes you wonder whether or not you could really outrun, say, a T-Rex, it turns out you probably could. In 2017, National Geographic reported on a series of simulations published in the journal PeerJ that estimated the mighty predator was very likely much slower than most people—including Steven Spielberg—ever imagined, with a top speed of only 12 miles per hour, down from previous estimates of as much as 30 miles per hour.

As one wet-blanket paleontologist put it, “No way T. rex could have chased down that Jeep in Jurassic Park if it was going at highway speeds,” he says. “Maybe if it was in first gear, but even that's a big if.” Forgive us, but we're going to have to ask Dr. Alan Grant for a second opinion.
