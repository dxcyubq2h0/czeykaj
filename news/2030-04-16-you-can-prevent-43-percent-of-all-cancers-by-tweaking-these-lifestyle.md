# You Can Prevent 43 Percent Of All Cancers By Tweaking These Lifestyle Habits

The key to cancer prevention might very well lie in your hands: According to new research by the American Cancer Society, factors potentially under your control, such as smoking, eating red and processed meats, and physical inactivity, contribute to a sizable chunk of new cancer cases.

In the study, researchers used established cancer risk factors and their relative risk—meaning how much they’re known to up your odds—to estimate the percentages of 26 different types of cancers that can be attributed to them. The risk factors include the following: cigarette smoking; secondhand smoke exposure; excess body weight; alcohol intake; consumption of red and processed meats; low consumption of fruits and vegetables, fiber, and calcium; physical inactivity; and UV exposure. They also included six infections which were linked to cancer, including human papillomavirus (HPV)—which is linked to oropharynx cancers—and human immunodeficiency virus (HIV), a major risk factor for Kaposi's sarcoma.

The most dangerous ones for guys? Cigarette smoking (attributable to 24 percent of all cancer cases), UV radiation exposure (6 percent of all cancer cases), and excess bodyweight (5 percent of all cancer cases). Excessive alcohol consumption and not eating enough fruits and vegetables rounded out the top 5 preventable cancer risk factors for men.

In men, smoking accounted for 84 percent of lung cancers, 75 percent of cancers of the larynx, and 52 percent of cancers of the esophagus. UV radiation was only linked to one cancer, but its effect was staggering—about 95 percent of all cases of melanoma could be attributed to it.

As for the other risk factors? The good news is, those factors are well under your control.
