A sprained ankle typically causes pain, swelling, and difficulty walking. A broken ankle, however, often results in severe pain, significant swelling, visible deformity, and an inability to bear weight on the affected foot.

Key differences between a sprained and broken ankle:

Sprain: Mild to moderate pain, gradual swelling, bruising, and limited but possible weight-bearing.

Fracture: Intense pain, immediate swelling, possible deformity, and inability to stand or walk.

Because symptoms can overlap, it's often difficult to tell the difference without a professional evaluation. Ignoring a fracture can lead to long-term complications, while treating a sprain incorrectly can delay healing.
