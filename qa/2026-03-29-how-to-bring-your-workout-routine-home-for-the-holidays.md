# How to Bring Your Workout Routine Home For The Holidays

The holidays are a perfect time to stuff yourself with mom's comfort food and make up for lost streaming binges, which also makes them the perfect time to fall off the wagon when it comes to your fitness routine and goals.

And that's the case even for the most dedicated among us, like celebrity trainer and Men's Health 2015 Next Top Trainer winner Gideon Akande. A first-generation American born to Nigerian immigrant parents, Akande visits his extended family every few years in West Africa. With visits up to five weeks at a time, he's become an expert at maintaining a fitness routine during long periods of travel (and comfort food galore).

Luckily, Akande says, a fitness routine is something you can maintain anywhere you go, no matter where or for how long. Here are a few simple tips.

During time away from home, it’s inevitable that your workout routine won't look exactly like it usually does. In Nigeria, Akande says, “I don’t know exactly where the local gyms are at, and those gyms don't have the facilities and services I’m used to.”

Instead of abandoning a workout completely, Akande makes do with what he has. “I just let the sweat flow and challenge myself,” he says. “I’m not looking to change my body or my life during those weeks; it's more maintenance as opposed to progress.”

A boxer first and foremost, Akande loves to increase the intensity of his workouts by facing off with a sparring partner, a heavy bag, or a barbell—and those aren’t always available.

“There are still ways I can exercise that are lower intensity,” he says. “There isn’t a boxer in the world who doesn’t shadow box. This way, you can get better without the impact of hitting, getting hit, or being in a real-life situation.”

After vacation is over? It’s time to ramp up again. “Sometimes you have to scale back so you can hit it harder later,” Akande says.

When dropping by the gym is truly impossible, Akande comes prepared: “There’s a handful of go-to items I take whenever I'm traveling,” he says, including a jump rope, resistance bands, and a weighted vest.

The key is to bring equipment that can be used anytime and anywhere, Akande says. “As long as you have a little space you can swing that rope around and throw on some music. And the weighted vest—you put it on and forget about it, but you've got an additional five, 10, or 20 pounds that forces your body to work harder.”

Even the most physically fit trainers can’t be perfect 100 percent of the time. “When it’s 10 in the morning and you have nowhere to be and want to lay in bed longer, that's tempting,” Akande says. “When you have the most delicious food mom has ever cooked and you want to eat everything—there’s so much out there and I allow it to distract me. I don’t beat myself up and not enjoy it, but I also make sure I’m being smart with how I do it.”

Akande recommends allowing yourself to indulge, but also being mindful and reasonable with what you’re enjoying. Instead of both overeating and oversleeping, allow yourself to do just one.

Holiday time is family time, Akande says, so while it’s important to keep track of your fitness goals, “don't be so unreasonable that you don’t have a life outside of exercise, too,” he says.

Even better? Invite friends and family in on the fun. “I encourage family and friends to join me,” says Akande. “I know what exercise does for me, so shame on me if I don't share that gift with other people, especially during the holidays.” It's also just more fun to stick to the routine when you have some company, Akande notes. Walking, jogging, and light stretching are his favorite ways to incorporate others.
