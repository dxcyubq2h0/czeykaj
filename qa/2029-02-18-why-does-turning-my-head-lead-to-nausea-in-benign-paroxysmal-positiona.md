Explanation

In Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo (BPPV), turning your head can lead to feelings of dizziness and nausea. This happens because BPPV is related to problems in the inner ear, which is responsible for balance. When you move your head, it can cause your balance system to send confusing signals to your brain, making you feel dizzy or sick to your stomach.\n\nUnderstanding BPPV and Nausea\n- Balance System: Your inner ear helps you stay balanced. When it doesn't work right, like in BPPV, it can make you feel like the world is spinning.\n- Motion Sickness Connection: The feeling is similar to motion sickness, where your brain gets mixed signals about movement.\n\nWhile the details of how BPPV causes nausea aren't fully covered in the references, they do discuss how issues with balance can lead to dizziness and nausea, much like what happens in motion sickness.
