Why Do Men Lose Hair?

Hair loss does not happen for just one reason. In most men, it is the result of several overlapping factors that affect the hair growth cycle and follicle health.

Genetics

Genetics is the most well known cause. Male pattern baldness, also called androgenetic alopecia, is inherited and driven by a sensitivity of hair follicles to dihydrotestosterone, or DHT. Over time, DHT causes follicles to shrink, leading to thinner, shorter hairs and eventually stopping growth altogether. (2)

Hormonal Changes

Hormonal changes also play a role in hair loss. Testosterone levels, DHT activity, thyroid hormones, and cortisol can all influence hair growth. (3) Hormonal imbalances may accelerate shedding or prevent healthy regrowth, especially when combined with genetics.

Aging

Aging also naturally affects hair. As men get older, hair growth slows, follicles spend more time in the resting phase, and strands may become finer. Even men without significant genetic hair loss often notice reduced density over time.

Stress and Illness

High stress levels can trigger temporary hair shedding known as telogen effluvium. (4) Additionally, medical conditions or procedures such as surgery, certain infections, or significant weight changes can also lead to telogen effluvium, causing noticeable thinning after a period of time, typically several months.

Nutritional Deficiencies and Medical Conditions

Hair follicles are metabolically active and require adequate protein, iron, zinc, biotin, and other micronutrients. Nutritional deficiencies can weaken hair overall and slow regrowth.Medical conditions such as thyroid disorders, autoimmune diseases, and scalp infections can also contribute to hair loss. (5) In these cases, treating the underlying condition is essential for improving hair health.

Lifestyle Changes That Support Hair Health

Lifestyle factors may not fully override genetics, but they can play a meaningful role in overall hair health and resilience. Supporting your body helps create the best possible environment for hair follicles to function. Here are some ways you may be able to slow down hair loss.

Balanced nutrition that includes adequate protein, healthy fats, vitamins, and minerals supports the hair growth cycle and follicle strength.

Stress management through exercise, mindfulness, and proper rest to reduce stress- related shedding.

Avoiding smoking to improve circulation and enhance oxygen delivery to hair follicles.

Gentle hair-care habits such as avoiding excessive heat, harsh chemicals, and tight hairstyles reduce unnecessary breakage and traction. (7)

When to Seek Professional Help

Many men wait until it’s too late when addressing hair loss. This can lead to relying on unproven solutions or simply hoping it will stabilize on its own. Knowing when to seek professional guidance and start treatment can make a substantial difference. Here are some key factors to look out for:

Hair loss appears to be accelerating or spreading beyond a typical pattern.

Thinning is accompanied by fatigue, weight changes, or other symptoms that may indicate a hormonal or medical issue.

Over-the-counter products have not produced results after several months of consistent use.

You want a clear diagnosis and evidence-based treatment options rather than relying on trial and error.

A professional evaluation helps identify the root cause of hair loss and rules out conditions that require medical treatment. It also helps prevent wasted time and money on ineffective or unsafe products.
