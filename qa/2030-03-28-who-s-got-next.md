# Who's Got Next?

Billiards

Get in the game: Play hard to get. "Don't talk to anybody; just go in there and shoot," says professional pool player Frank Alvarez. No one can resist challenging the stranger in town.

Play it right: Lag for the break; it's cool. Avoid "sharking"--clowning while your opponent is shooting. And one more rule: "Slow down on the backswing," says Jeanette "Black Widow" Lee.

Get in the game: "A guy who doesn't know the game rules can mess up the run," says Grayson "Professor" Boucher, a professional street-baller with the AND 1 Mix-Tape Tour. Know the scoring system (often to 11, win by 2), and in half-court whether it's make-it/ take-it.

Play it right: Work hard on defense, pass often, don't trash-talk, and unless you're spitting bloody Chiclets, don't call a foul.

Darts

Get in the game: Most busy bars use a chalkboard at peak hours to schedule games; if it's slow, just ask.

Play it right: Know the games, like x01 and Cricket (rules at a1darts.com). Keep mum until all three darts are thrown, stay at least 3 feet behind the shooter, and never touch your darts until they've been tallied. "Finish each throw with your hand pointed at your target" as though reaching out to touch it, says Leslie Murphy, executive director of the AMOA National Dart Association.

Chess

Get in the game: Don't be sandbagged, warns James Eade, author of Chess for Dummies. If the games are played with a clock (5 minutes is typical), ask for time odds against a better player: "Want to give me 5-to-4 odds?"

Play it right: Don't hold your hand over the board without touching a piece. "It's a dead giveaway" that you're a rookie, says Eade.
