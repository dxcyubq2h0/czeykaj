People at the highest risk for severe flesh-eating bacteria (necrotizing fasciitis) infections include those with weakened immune systems, chronic health conditions such as diabetes, kidney disease, liver disease, or cancer, and individuals with open wounds, recent surgery, or skin injuries. Older adults and people who use IV drugs are also more vulnerable. Because these infections progress rapidly and can become life-threatening within hours, early recognition of symptoms like severe pain, swelling, redness, or fever is critical.

Explanation

Flesh-eating bacteria infections, also known as necrotizing fasciitis, can be very serious. According to the references, people who are at higher risk include those with weak immune systems or other health issues.

Key Risk Factors

Weakened Immune System: People with weaker immune systems, such as those with chronic illnesses or on certain medications, are more vulnerable.

Existing Health Problems: Conditions like diabetes or liver disease can make it easier for these infections to take hold.

Open Wounds or Cuts: Having an open wound or cut can provide an entry point for bacteria.

Soil and Water Exposure: As noted in the references, exposure to soil or water where the bacteria live can increase risk.
